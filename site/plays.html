How this works

What it does. For a given down, distance, field position, call and offensive personnel group, the model predicts the probability of every yard result on the snap. It sees only what is known before the snap: the situation, the personnel and formation on the field, the two teams' ratings, and the weather.

What the bars mean. Each bar is the chance of that exact gain. The bars to the right of the dashed line reach the line to gain. A touchdown is shown as its own bar because it is not a number of yards. Turnover chance comes from a separate model and overlaps the yard bars, so it is drawn as an outline.

What is held fixed. Everything not on the console is set to a neutral value (tied game, mid second quarter, average teams, mild weather), so you compare situations rather than games.

How it was tested. The model was scored once on the 2020 to 2025 seasons, using only earlier seasons to train, against a baseline that knows only down, distance and field position. The score is CRPS, the average miss of the whole yard distribution; lower is better.
